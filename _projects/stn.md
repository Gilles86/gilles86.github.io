During my PhD with Birte Forstmann at the University of Amsterdam, I studied small
subcortical structures that are hard to image, in particular the *subthalamic
nucleus (STN)*, using 7 Tesla MRI, histology and computational models like the
drift diffusion model. Much of the human subcortex is still poorly understood
([Forstmann et al., 2017, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2016.163)).

 * **Iron changes gradually within the STN.** Iron increases gradually towards its medial-inferior tip, as we showed with quantitative susceptibility mapping in vivo and post mortem ([de Hollander\*, Keuken\* et al., 2014, *Human Brain Mapping*](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.22485)).
 * **Reported STN activity is often misplaced.** Reported ''STN'' activations are often a mix of the STN, the substantia nigra and their surroundings ([de Hollander\*, Keuken\* et al., 2015, *PLOS ONE*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0120572)), and basal ganglia fMRI needs dedicated protocols at 7T ([de Hollander et al., 2017, *Human Brain Mapping*](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.23586)).
 * **The STN shows gradients, not discrete parts.** Histological markers show gradients rather than discrete subdivisions ([Alkemade\*, de Hollander\*, Miletić\* et al., 2019, *Brain Structure and Function*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00429-019-01960-3)).
